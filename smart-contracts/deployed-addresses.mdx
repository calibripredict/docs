Every contract Calibri trades against is public. You do not have to take custody
claims on trust: read the code, check the balances, and confirm your own Safe
holds what you think it holds.

This page lists what is deployed and shows you how to verify each address for
yourself.

<Note>
  The **API is the authoritative source** for every address on every network. The values below are for reference and verification — if this page and the API ever disagree, the API is right and this page is stale.
</Note>

The CTF Exchange was deployed on **7 October 2026** and carries Calibri's own
EIP-712 domain name, **Calibri CTF Prediction Markets** — it is not a shared or
third-party exchange. That domain name is hashed into every order signature, which is
why the app serves it rather than hard-coding it, and why signatures made against a
different exchange cannot be replayed here.

<Note>
  **ConditionalTokens is Gnosis' code, not ours.** The instance above is deployed from Gnosis' published, audited Conditional Token Framework build, unmodified — its bytecode is identical to that build, which you can compare on Polygonscan. The contract has no owner and no admin function: nobody, Calibri included, can move or freeze the collateral it holds; only the holder of an outcome token can redeem it.
</Note>

<Note>
  Calibri's earlier exchange, `0xB6Ac44d5A6388b8C7511F2fb20C0c03fD0CeDC83`, is retired: paused, with no admin or operator left. Nothing trades or settles against it.
</Note>

## Wallet infrastructure

These four are what a **Safe** is made of. You never need them to trade, or to move funds
from a Safe that exists — they matter for exactly one case: a Safe address that was funded
before it was deployed. Deployment happens on your first deposit, so an address can hold
USDC with no contract at it yet, and both factories below are **permissionless**, meaning
anyone can deploy it. That is what keeps
[withdrawing without Calibri](/non-custodial/withdraw-without-calibri) true even for a
wallet we never got around to creating.

| Contract | Address | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe factory** (CalibriSafeFactoryV2) | `0x5f26b4D72A858b9F1441d0241adF01D6A23f1894` | Deploys your Safe at its derived address |
| **Safe singleton** (Safe 1.3.0) | `0xd9Db270c1B5E3Bd161E8c8503c55cEABeE709552` | The implementation every Safe proxy delegatecalls |
| **Safe fallback handler** (Safe 1.3.0) | `0xf48f2B2d2a534e402487b3ee7C18c33Aec0Fe5e4` | Handles signature checks and other non-core calls |
| **WebAuthn signer factory** | `0x2508Ef28544e00392a544A062ab3D2334F0C0d5a` | Deploys the secp256r1 signer contract a passkey owns |

<Note>
  The singleton and the fallback handler are Safe's own canonical 1.3.0 deployments, used by Safes across every network. All four are fixed per network, identical for every member and not secret: the factory is also readable from the exchange's own `safeFactory` view, and the singleton and handler from any deployed Safe's storage.
</Note>

## Cross-network deposits

These contracts let USDC sent to your Safe address on another network reach your Safe on
Polygon — see [Deposits from other networks](/non-custodial/deposits-from-other-networks).
Each is deployed at **the same address on Polygon and Ethereum**, which is what makes your
Safe's address the same on both. **CalibriSafeFactoryV2** is the Safe factory listed above.

| Contract | Address | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **CalibriSafeFactoryV2** | `0x5f26b4D72A858b9F1441d0241adF01D6A23f1894` | Creates member Safes with the bridge module enabled; permissionless — [Polygonscan](https://polygonscan.com/address/0x5f26b4D72A858b9F1441d0241adF01D6A23f1894) · [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/address/0x5f26b4D72A858b9F1441d0241adF01D6A23f1894) |
| **SafeModuleSetup** | `0x389CB393c51373244fC67A1a08f9f9aB0b5323BB` | Switches the module on during a Safe's setup — [Polygonscan](https://polygonscan.com/address/0x389CB393c51373244fC67A1a08f9f9aB0b5323BB) · [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/address/0x389CB393c51373244fC67A1a08f9f9aB0b5323BB) |
| **CctpBridgeModule** | `0x1441E0De9aCDBf3bb0D527f598219BF0403eF3A2` | Moves a Safe's USDC through Circle's CCTP to the same Safe on Polygon — [Polygonscan](https://polygonscan.com/address/0x1441E0De9aCDBf3bb0D527f598219BF0403eF3A2) · [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/address/0x1441E0De9aCDBf3bb0D527f598219BF0403eF3A2) |
| **BridgeFeeConfig** | `0x7093AF674b82b9f8cFFd4f9bC74ac7b0DaB4a773` | Holds the relayer allowed to charge the bridge fee, the treasury it is paid to, and the consent signer that countersigns an accepted fee above the cap — [Polygonscan](https://polygonscan.com/address/0x7093AF674b82b9f8cFFd4f9bC74ac7b0DaB4a773) · [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/address/0x7093AF674b82b9f8cFFd4f9bC74ac7b0DaB4a773) |

<Note>
  **Ethereum is the only other network live today.** USDC sent to your Safe address on any other network is not bridged — send USDC on Polygon or Ethereum only.
</Note>

You can check a module against its limits yourself. `CctpBridgeModule`
exposes `MAX_FEE_ABS`, `MAX_FEE_BPS`, `MIN_AMOUNT`, `DEST_DOMAIN` (`7`, Polygon) and
`TOKEN_MESSENGER` (Circle's TokenMessengerV2) as public views, and `localUsdc()` returns the
USDC it moves on that network. `CalibriSafeFactoryV2.module()` names the module every Safe
is created with, and `computeProxyAddress(owner)` returns your Safe's address — the same
answer on every network.

## Reading the live values

Signed in, these two endpoints return the exact addresses your account is
trading against:

```
GET /api/v2/atlas/account/wallet
GET /api/v2/atlas/account/wallet/markets/:id
```

The member-level call returns `chain_id`, `blockchain_key`, `usdc_address`,
`conditional_tokens_address`, `exchange_address`, `domain_name`, your own
`proxy_address` (your Safe), and `owner_address` (what owns it). The per-market
call adds `condition_id`, `yes_token_id`, and `no_token_id` — the two ERC-1155
outcome tokens for that market.

<Warning>
  Read these addresses from the API rather than copying them into your own client. A wrong address silently sends signatures somewhere they cannot settle.
</Warning>

## Verifying it yourself

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check your Safe holds your money">
    Take `proxy_address` from `GET /account/wallet` and open it on Polygonscan. Its USDC
    balance is your money, in a contract only you can authorise. Nothing about
    that depends on Calibri being honest.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the exchange you are signing for">
    Confirm `exchange_address` from the API matches the CTF Exchange above, and
    that the contract has code at that address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the collateral">
    Confirm `usdc_address` is native USDC on Polygon — the address in the table
    above — and not a look-alike token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check a specific market">
    For any market, verify its `condition_id` and the `yes_token_id` /
    `no_token_id` against ConditionalTokens. Those token ids are derived from the
    resolver, the question and the collateral, so they name exactly one outcome
    of one market and never change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch a settlement land">
    After a market resolves, the redemption that pays you is a transaction on
    ConditionalTokens against your Safe. It is on the explorer like anything
    else.
  </Step>
</Steps>
