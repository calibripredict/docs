Every trade on Calibri starts as a signed order — a cryptographic commitment to buy or sell a specific quantity of an outcome token at a specified price. Calibri supports five distinct order types, each suited to a different trading strategy. Once submitted, an order moves through a well-defined lifecycle from placement to final onchain settlement. Understanding order types and lifecycle states helps you execute precisely and manage risk effectively.

## Order Types

Choose the order type that matches your execution intent before you submit. Each type controls how aggressively Calibri tries to fill your order and what happens to any unfilled remainder.

| Type | Description | Best used when… |
|---|---|---|
| **GTC** (Good Till Cancelled) | Rests on the order book indefinitely until fully filled or manually cancelled | You want to set a limit price and wait for the market to come to you |
| **GTD** (Good Till Date) | Like GTC, but automatically expires at a specified Unix timestamp | You want a limit order with a hard expiration — e.g., before an event ends |
| **FOK** (Fill Or Kill) | Must be filled in its entirety immediately; cancelled outright if full quantity is unavailable | You need a guaranteed complete fill or no fill at all |
| **FAK** (Fill And Kill) | Fills as much as possible immediately; any unfilled remainder is cancelled | You want immediate execution but are comfortable with a partial fill |
| **Post-Only** | Rejected if it would cross the spread; guaranteed to rest as a maker order | You want to earn maker rebates and will never accept taker fees |

<Warning>
  FOK orders are all-or-nothing. If the order book cannot satisfy the **entire** requested quantity at your limit price at the moment of submission, Calibri cancels the whole order — no partial fill is ever returned. Use FAK instead if a partial fill is acceptable.
</Warning>

## Order Signing

Calibri orders are **EIP-712 signed messages**. You sign the order data with your wallet's private key, and the resulting signature is submitted to the Calibri API alongside the order parameters. Your tokens never leave your wallet until the moment a trade settles onchain — Calibri never takes custody.

A signed order object contains the following key fields:

```json
{
  "tokenId": "52114319501245915516055106046884209969926127482827954674443846427813813222426",
  "makerAmount": "65000000",
  "takerAmount": "100000000",
  "side": "BUY",
  "expiration": "0",
  "orderType": "GTC",
  "signature": "0x4a6f2b8c...d3e1f09a",
  "signatureType": 0
}
```

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `tokenId` | ERC-1155 token ID of the outcome token you are trading (Yes or No) |
| `makerAmount` | Amount you commit to provide, in base units (6 decimals for pUSD) |
| `takerAmount` | Amount you expect to receive, in base units |
| `side` | `BUY` to acquire outcome tokens; `SELL` to dispose of them |
| `expiration` | Unix timestamp after which the order is void; `0` = no expiration (GTC) |
| `orderType` | One of `GTC`, `GTD`, `FOK`, `FAK`, `POST_ONLY` |
| `signature` | EIP-712 signature bytes |
| `signatureType` | Signature scheme — `0` for EOA, `1` for EIP-1271 smart contract wallets |

The `makerAmount` / `takerAmount` ratio implicitly encodes your limit price. For a BUY order at $0.65 for 100 tokens: `makerAmount = 65000000` (65 pUSD), `takerAmount = 100000000` (100 Yes tokens).

## Order Lifecycle

An order progresses through the following statuses after submission:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Delayed">
    Immediately after submission, some orders enter a brief asynchronous delay window. During this window the order is received but not yet visible on the book. This protects the system against front-running in high-throughput scenarios.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Live (Unmatched)">
    After the delay window (or immediately for orders that skip it), the order rests on the order book as `live`. It is visible to all other participants as a bid or ask. This status persists until the order is filled, cancelled, or expires.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Matched">
    The matching engine has found a counterparty and executed a trade. The order status becomes `matched`. From here, the trade moves through its own sub-lifecycle before onchain settlement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once an order is `matched`, the resulting **trade** progresses through these statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `MATCHED` | Trade is agreed between maker and taker; awaiting onchain submission |
| `MINED` | The settlement transaction has been submitted and included in a block |
| `CONFIRMED` | The transaction has reached sufficient block confirmations — final |
| `RETRYING` | Onchain submission failed; the system is re-attempting settlement |
| `FAILED` | Settlement could not be completed after retries; contact support |

## Maker vs. Taker Flow

When you submit an order, Calibri's matching engine determines your role:

- **Taker flow**: your order price crosses the current best opposite quote. The engine matches you immediately against the resting order. Your order skips the `live` state and goes directly to `matched`.
- **Maker flow**: your order price does not cross any resting quote. Your order enters `live` state and waits. When a future taker crosses your price, the engine matches both orders and both transition to `matched`.

Makers typically receive fee rebates for providing liquidity. Takers pay a small fee for the convenience of immediate execution. Use **Post-Only** orders to guarantee you always act as a maker.

## BUY vs. SELL

The `side` field controls which direction you are trading:

**BUY** — you are acquiring outcome tokens. Buying Yes tokens means you believe the outcome will happen. Buying No tokens means you believe it will not happen. In both cases you spend pUSD to receive tokens.

**SELL** — you are disposing of outcome tokens you already hold. Selling Yes tokens reduces your long position on that outcome. Selling No tokens reduces your short position. In both cases you spend tokens to receive pUSD.

A common pattern: buy Yes early at a low price, then sell Yes later at a higher price to lock in profit — without waiting for the market to resolve.

## Auto-Cancel (Dead Man's Switch)

Algorithmic traders and market makers often maintain many resting orders simultaneously. If your system goes offline unexpectedly, those orders could fill at stale prices. Calibri's **auto-cancel** mechanism prevents this.

You register a heartbeat with the CLOB API at a regular interval, signaling that your system is still online. If the heartbeat lapses — because your server crashed or lost connectivity — Calibri automatically cancels every resting order associated with your API key.

Configure the heartbeat interval to be shorter than your worst-case acceptable stale-order exposure. For example, a 30-second heartbeat means your orders will be cancelled within 30 seconds of a connectivity loss.
