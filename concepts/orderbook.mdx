Calibri uses a **Central Limit Order Book (CLOB)** to match buyers and sellers of outcome tokens directly, peer-to-peer. Unlike automated market makers (AMMs) that rely on liquidity pools and bonding curves, the CLOB reflects true supply and demand: every trade is a voluntary exchange between two counterparties at a price they both agreed to. This produces tighter spreads, more accurate prices, and better execution for active traders.

## What Is a CLOB?

A Central Limit Order Book is a real-time ledger of all open buy and sell orders for a given market. When you submit an order, Calibri's matching engine checks whether any existing order on the opposite side satisfies your price. If a match exists, your order fills immediately. If not, your order rests on the book until a counterparty arrives — or until you cancel it.

Key properties of Calibri's CLOB:

- **No market maker required.** Any participant can provide liquidity by placing resting limit orders.
- **No custody.** Orders are EIP-712 signed messages. Your tokens stay in your wallet until the moment a trade settles onchain.
- **Continuous matching.** The engine processes orders in real time, so fills can happen any time the market is active.

## Bids and Asks

Every market's order book has two sides:

- **Bids** — open buy orders, sorted highest price first. The highest bid is the most a buyer is currently willing to pay.
- **Asks** — open sell orders, sorted lowest price first. The lowest ask is the least a seller is currently willing to accept.

The **spread** is the gap between the highest bid and the lowest ask. A narrow spread signals high liquidity; a wide spread signals thin liquidity.

Here is an example order book snapshot for a Yes token:

```json
{
  "bids": [
    { "price": "0.64", "size": "100" },
    { "price": "0.63", "size": "250" }
  ],
  "asks": [
    { "price": "0.66", "size": "75" },
    { "price": "0.67", "size": "150" }
  ],
  "last_trade_price": "0.65",
  "tick_size": "0.01",
  "min_order_size": "5"
}
```

In this example, the spread is $0.02 (ask $0.66 − bid $0.64). The last trade occurred at $0.65.

## Price Discovery

Calibri displays a single **mid-price** for each market outcome, computed as:

```text
mid-price = (best bid + best ask) / 2
```

Using the example above: `(0.64 + 0.66) / 2 = 0.65`.

This mid-price doubles as a **probability estimate**. A Yes token mid-price of $0.65 means the aggregate of all open orders implies a 65% chance that the outcome resolves Yes. As new information enters the market, traders update their orders and the mid-price shifts accordingly.

If the spread exceeds $0.10, Calibri falls back to the **last traded price** instead of the midpoint, since an unusually wide spread can produce a misleading mid-price.

<Note>
  When a new market launches with no trading history, the order book is empty and there is no mid-price yet. Liquidity bootstraps organically: the first traders to notice that buying Yes + buying No totals less than $1.00 can split pUSD and sell both sides at a profit, seeding the book and tightening the spread.
</Note>

## Tick Size and Min Order Size

Two parameters constrain how you can place orders in every market:

**Tick size** is the minimum price increment. A tick size of `$0.01` means valid prices are $0.01, $0.02, …, $0.99. You cannot place an order at $0.645 — it would need to be $0.64 or $0.65. Calibri enforces tick size at order submission and rejects non-conforming prices.

**Min order size** is the minimum quantity (in outcome tokens) per order. An order below the minimum is rejected outright. This floor reduces book spam and keeps the matching engine efficient.

Both values are returned in the order book response (`tick_size` and `min_order_size`) and can also be fetched from the Markets API.

## Maker vs. Taker

Every trade involves two roles:

| Role | Definition | Effect on book |
|---|---|---|
| **Maker** | Places an order that rests on the book | Adds liquidity |
| **Taker** | Places an order that immediately crosses with a resting order | Removes liquidity |

When you submit a limit order priced away from the current best, it becomes a maker order and sits on the book. When you submit an order priced at or through the best opposite-side quote, it executes immediately as a taker order.

Makers may earn **fee rebates** for providing liquidity, while takers typically pay a small fee. The exact fee schedule is published in the Calibri fee documentation.

To guarantee your order always rests as a maker, use the **Post-Only** order type — Calibri will reject the order if it would immediately cross the spread rather than silently converting it to a taker.

## Negative Risk and the Order Book

In a **negative risk group** (a set of mutually exclusive markets), the order books of all member markets are economically linked. Holding a No token in one market is equivalent to holding a Yes token in every other market in the group.

This linkage creates natural cross-market arbitrage: traders can simultaneously quote across multiple markets in the group and hedge their exposure with a single No position. The result is tighter spreads and deeper liquidity across the entire event, compared to treating each market as completely independent.

When you trade in a negative risk market, the Calibri contracts handle the token accounting automatically — you do not need to manually convert between No tokens and the equivalent basket of Yes tokens.
