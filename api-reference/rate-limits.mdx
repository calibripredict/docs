---
title: "Rate Limits"
description: "Per-endpoint request allowances, how limits are keyed, and how to back off correctly when you hit one."
---

Limits are applied per endpoint class rather than as one global budget, so a
burst of order placement cannot exhaust the allowance you need to sign in, and a
scripted retry loop on one endpoint cannot lock you out of the rest of the API.

Every limit below is enforced server-side and is not configurable per account.

## What you get back

A throttled request returns **`429 Too Many Requests`**. Per-IP identity limits
tag the body with `identity.too_many_requests`. Per-user identity limits return
their own code: `identity.session.otp_rate_limited`,
`identity.phones.send_code_rate_limited`, `identity.phones.verify_rate_limited`,
`identity.email.send_rate_limited`, `identity.email.verify_rate_limited` or
`identity.email.confirmation_rate_limited`, each
with a `Retry-After` header.

The `/atlas` limits below also send three headers on every counted request,
throttled or not:

| Header | Value |
|---|---|
| `X-RateLimit-Limit` | The allowance for this window |
| `X-RateLimit-Remaining` | Requests left in it |
| `X-RateLimit-Reset` | Unix time (seconds) when the window resets |

The `/atlas` routes send no `Retry-After`; read `X-RateLimit-Reset` instead. The `/pythia`
order limit sends the same three headers and also `Retry-After` (seconds) on a
`429`. Its windows are
fixed, starting at your first counted request, and have no extra block period —
the allowance is back as soon as the window ends.

<Warning>
  Several identity limits **block for longer than the window they measure**. Ten sign-in requests in a minute do not free up after that minute: the eleventh starts a five-minute block. Requests made during a block are refused and do not extend it; see [Backing off](#backing-off).
</Warning>

## Sign-in and account creation

Keyed **per IP address**, so these apply before anyone is authenticated.

| Endpoints | Allowance | Blocked for |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /identity/sessions`<br/>`POST /identity/sessions/social`<br/>`POST /identity/sessions/web3/nonce`<br/>`POST /identity/sessions/web3` | 10 per minute | 5 minutes |
| `POST /identity/users`<br/>`POST /identity/users/password/generate_code`<br/>`POST /identity/users/email/generate_code` | 5 per minute | 5 minutes |
| `POST /identity/users/password/confirm_code`<br/>`POST /identity/users/email/confirm_code` | 10 per minute | 15 minutes |

Note the asymmetry between requesting a code and submitting one: requesting is
capped at 5/minute per IP because each request sends an email (and at one confirmation
email a minute per account — see below), while submitting is
capped at 10/minute with a **longer** block, because that endpoint is where a
token would be brute-forced.

## Two-factor and phone verification

Keyed **per user**, and enforced after authentication.

| Action | Allowance | Blocked for |
|---|---|---|
| 2FA code on sign-in | 5 per minute | 15 minutes |
| `POST /resource/phones`<br/>`POST /resource/phones/send_code` | 3 per hour | 1 hour |
| `POST /resource/phones/verify` | 5 per 15 minutes | 1 hour |
| Confirmation email: `POST /identity/users/email/generate_code`, and the email resent when an unconfirmed account signs in | 1 per minute | 1 minute |

<Note>
  The 2FA budget is **shared across all three sign-in paths** — email, social and wallet. Alternating between them does not give you three separate allowances.
</Note>

The confirmation-email allowance is one budget for both ways of sending it. Over it,
`generate_code` answers `429 identity.email.confirmation_rate_limited`; a sign-in by
the unconfirmed account still answers as usual but sends no new email.

Sending an SMS costs money and reaching a real handset, so 3 per hour is
deliberately tight. Build the "resend code" button to respect it: a user who taps
resend three times in a minute is locked out of SMS for the rest of the hour.

## Trading and wallet

Keyed **per member**.

| Endpoints | Allowance |
|---|---|
| `POST /atlas/account/orders` (signed orders)<br/>`POST /atlas/account/orders/batch`<br/>`POST /atlas/account/orders/{id}/replace` | 600 per minute, **shared**; a batch counts one per order in it |
| `POST /pythia/orders/{id}/cancel`<br/>`POST /pythia/orders/cancel` | 600 per minute, **shared** by both |
| `POST /atlas/account/wallet/relay` | 30 per 5 minutes |
| `POST /atlas/account/wallet/passkey`<br/>`POST /atlas/account/wallet/owners`<br/>`POST /atlas/account/wallet/session-key`<br/>`DELETE /atlas/account/wallet/session-key`<br/>`POST /atlas/account/market_requests` | 5 per hour, per URL |
| `POST /atlas/account/bridge/transfers/{id}/consent` | 10 per minute |
| `GET /atlas/account/bridge/withdraw/quote` | 10 per minute |
| `POST /atlas/account/onramp/checkout-sessions` | 20 per 10 minutes |
| `GET /atlas/account/onramp/checkout-sessions/{uid}` | 60 per 10 minutes, across all checkouts |

Order placement is the loosest limit on the API at ten per second sustained,
because it is the one endpoint where a legitimate client is genuinely fast.

The signed-order budget on `/atlas` and the engine budget on `/pythia` are
**separate**. Placing a signed order counts against the `/atlas` budget only;
cancelling it counts against the `/pythia` one. A replace counts as one order
on `/atlas` and nothing on `/pythia`, so re-quoting by replace costs half what
cancel-then-place does. A batch of 20 orders counts 20; a batch over the
remaining budget is refused whole, never partly placed, and leaves the rest
of the budget untouched. A client that re-quotes by
cancel-then-place spends one engine request per cancel. A throttled engine request returns `429`
with `market.order.rate_limited`.

The 5-per-hour class is counted **per URL**, not per method: `POST` and `DELETE`
on `/wallet/session-key` are the same URL and share one bucket of 5, while
`/wallet/passkey`, `/wallet/owners`, `/wallet/session-key` and
`/market_requests` each have their own.

The relay limit is tighter than it looks because **the operator pays the gas** for
every relayed transaction — it is a spend limit as much as a rate limit. The
5-per-hour class covers enrolment and one-off setup actions, which no correct
client repeats in a loop.

## Market data

Public market-data reads are **not currently rate-limited**, but they are cached
for **5 to 15 seconds** — 5 on live data such as order books, depth, the trade
tape and tickers, 15 on browse data such as events and markets. See
[Caching](/api-reference/introduction#caching) in the API overview.
Polling inside the TTL returns the same bytes and gains you nothing.

<Warning>
  Do not treat "no limit today" as a guarantee. Use the [WebSocket](/api-reference/websockets) for anything live; it is both cheaper for you and the reason this endpoint class has not needed a limit.
</Warning>

## Backing off

Retry with **exponential backoff and jitter**, and treat the block duration — not
the measurement window — as your floor.

```
attempt 1 fails  ->  wait  1s ± jitter
attempt 2 fails  ->  wait  2s
attempt 3 fails  ->  wait  4s
attempt 4 fails  ->  wait  8s   ... capped at 60s
```

Three rules that matter more than the curve:

- **Add jitter.** Without it, every client that failed together retries together, and the retry storm is worse than the original burst.
- **Never retry a 429 immediately**, even once. On the endpoints with a block, an immediate retry lands inside the block and is refused.
- **Do not retry a `422`**, with three exceptions. It is a validation failure, and the same body will fail every time — except `market.order.tokens_settling`, a sell of shares still settling on-chain, which succeeds once the match confirms, and `Unable to verify member safe readiness` and `Unable to verify signed_order signature`, which mean a check against the chain could not be completed. Otherwise only `429`, `5xx` and network errors are worth retrying.

## If you need more

Order placement at 600/minute is sized for a quoting client. If you are
building something that genuinely needs more — a market maker quoting across many
markets — raise a support ticket at
