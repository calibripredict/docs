description: "Placing a signed limit order, choosing YES or NO, and how a fill becomes a position."
---

An order is your instruction to trade a market. On Calibri you **buy the outcome
you believe in** — there is no separate "buy" and "sell": you pick **YES** or
**NO**, a price, and a size. This page covers how orders work in practice. For the
underlying model see [Core Concepts](/concepts/core-concepts).

## What you specify

Every order has:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Market** | The market (event) you are trading. |
| **Side** | **`yes`** or **`no`** — the outcome you are buying. Not "buy/sell". |
| **Order type** | **Limit**. Every order carries your signature, which commits to a price. |
| **Volume** | Number of contracts. |
| **Price** | Price per contract, `0.01`–`0.99`. |

<Note>
  On the API these map to the order fields `market`, `side` (`"yes"`/`"no"`), `ord_type` (`"limit"`), `volume`, and `price`. See the [API Overview](/api-reference/introduction) for the exact request shape.
</Note>

## Limit orders

A **limit order** sets the exact price you're willing to pay and rests in the order
book until someone trades against it (or you cancel it).

```
You place: BUY 100 YES, limit, price 0.60
→ If there's a matching NO order at 0.40, you fill immediately.
→ Otherwise your order rests in the book at 0.60 until it's matched or cancelled.
```

- **Pros:** exact price control, no slippage; a resting order can earn a
  [maker rebate](/concepts/rewards) when it's the side that gets filled.
- **Cons:** it may not fill if the price never reaches your limit.

A `post_only` limit order is **rejected if it would immediately take liquidity** —
use it when you only want to rest in the book and never cross the spread.

## Why there are no market orders

Every order you place is signed by your own key, and the signature commits to a
specific price — that is what lets the exchange match your order without ever
holding your funds. A market order names no price, so there would be nothing to
sign.

To fill immediately, place a limit order at or through the best resting price:
a buy at `0.62` when the best ask is `0.60` fills at `0.60`, not `0.62`. You get
immediate execution and still cap what you can pay.

## The combined order book

Calibri runs **one combined book** per market — there is no separate NO book. A
trade happens when a YES buyer and a NO buyer are matched and their prices sum to
`1.00`. So a resting YES bid at `0.60` is simultaneously offering the NO side at
`0.40`; you never have to hunt for "the other side."

## Fees

You pay a single **taker fee** only when your order **takes** liquidity (a market
order, or a limit order that crosses the spread and fills immediately). Resting
orders that get filled are **makers** and pay no fee. The fee is charged **once,
at the moment of the fill — never again at settlement**. It follows a
price-dependent bell curve rather than a flat percentage; see
[Fees](/concepts/fees) for the exact formula and how the applicable rate is shown
for each market.

## How your order reaches the market

Every order is **EIP-712 signed** by your wallet — or by your passkey — and
relayed on-chain via the orchestrator. **The operator pays the gas**, so signing
costs you nothing.

Signing is what authorises the order against your Safe; nothing can be placed on
your behalf without it. See [Signed orders](/non-custodial/signed-orders) for the
exact struct and [Your wallet options](/non-custodial/wallets) for what does the
signing in each setup.

## From fill to position

Once an order fills:

- A **contract** is created between your side and the matched counterparty. Your
  **position** is the set of contract legs you hold (net YES, net NO, or flat).
- Positions are **held to resolution**. Calibri does not currently support
  selling a position back, and buying the opposite side does not close the first
  one — it opens a second, separately collateralised position, locking more
  money rather than releasing any.
- Open (unfilled) limit orders can be **cancelled** while the market is open; the
  collateral they locked is released back to you.

At resolution, winning contracts pay **1.00 USDC** per share and losing contracts
pay **0**, with no settlement fee.
